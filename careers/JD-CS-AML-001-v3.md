## 1. About Aras Integrasi and ChainSentry

Aras Integrasi Sdn Bhd is a Malaysian technology and security integrator with an established government and regulated-industry client base, including national security-agency deployments. **ChainSentry** is the company's sovereign blockchain-forensics and E-KYC product line, built for governments, financial-intelligence units (FIUs), banks and licensed virtual-asset service providers (VASPs) that US-cloud-incumbent vendors price out, cannot serve on data-residency grounds, or leave blind on Gulf–Southeast Asia–BRICS correspondent corridors.

Three principles define the product and are the standard against which this role's outputs are judged:

- **Sovereign deployment.** A customer's cases never leave the customer's jurisdiction. The product runs on-premises or in a jurisdiction-locked hosted instance the customer can audit.
- **Corridor coverage.** Sourced, measurable attribution depth on the USDT/TRON Gulf-to-SEA off-ramp graph and adjacent MENA/SEA corridors — the graphs the incumbents underlabel.
- **Auditability.** Detection methodology, label provenance and per-corridor precision/recall are published, not proprietary. Every label is expected to be defensible in the customer's court.

The product family now spans three layers: ChainSentry forensics, chain:HARVEST wallet-intelligence harvesting (a continuously updated corpus of tens of thousands of checksum-validated wallet attributions drawn from dark-web, Telegram, ransomware-leak and sanctions sources, with an embedded OFAC screening engine), and an identity-assurance layer drawing on a university E-KYC research pipeline (tamper-resistant document authentication and liveness detection). A design-partner cohort of ~120 candidate institutions is being assembled across Malaysia, Vietnam, Thailand, the Philippines, Indonesia, Singapore, the UAE, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Jordan, Turkey, Egypt, Lebanon, Morocco and Switzerland — regulators and FIUs, licensed exchanges and e-money institutions, banks and tokenisation platforms, and professional-services channels.

**Why this role exists:** ChainSentry's go-to-market is compliance-led. Supervisors and supervised entities adopt a tracing tool only when the methodology is defensible before their courts, the data handling satisfies their laws, and the vendor can sit across the table from an examiner as a credible peer. Today that discipline is carried informally by engineering and the product lead. This role makes it a function: pilots become lawful, evidence-preserving engagements; the attribution corpus stays defensible; regulators are answered in their own language. It does not replace sales — it makes sales compliant and pilots defensible.

## 2. About the Role

You will be the named AML/CFT subject-matter expert of a sovereign blockchain-forensics product in market. You will own the compliance posture of the design-partner programme, the sanctions and watchlist integrity of the attribution corpus, and the company's written and spoken presence before regulators, FIUs and law-enforcement financial-crime units — while staying a hands-on practitioner: casework, screening, drafting. You will operate as the named officer of record for financial-crime substance in this product line, reporting to the product lead with a commercial dotted line to the COO.

## 3. Key Responsibilities

### 3.1 Compliance-by-design for pilots and design partners
- Draft, negotiate and administer the design-partner agreement suite: pilot term sheets, written-reference and quotation clauses, sanitised labelled-case contribution terms, and the data-rights clause that treats a pilot's labelled dispositions as paid consideration.
- Maintain the pilot compliance matrix — legal basis, cross-border data categories, hosting location, retention and destruction/return obligations — for every active pilot.
- Pre-empt jurisdiction blockers as a habit: Malaysian PDPA and AMLA 2001 handling for law-enforcement and reporting-institution data; Swiss banking-secrecy treatment for FIU-type submissions; UAE federal and emirate-level rules across VARA and SCA regimes; Vietnamese and Egyptian pilot-documentation obligations.
- Run pre-launch compliance reviews with engineering and close-out reviews that certify what corpus material may lawfully return to the shared corpus, in what sanitised form, under which clause.

### 3.2 Crypto AML operations: sanctions and corpus integrity
- Own the sanctioned-address and watchlist labelling pipeline end to end: OFAC SDN, EU, UK (OFSI) and UN lists, court-document addresses, ransomware-payment trackers and takedown archives converted into deterministic, versioned, source-cited seed labels.
- Define label acceptance criteria and QA machine-generated clusters; nothing enters the production corpus without a provenance chain that survives the question "where did this attribution come from?"
- Operate and tune the sanctions-screening engine for corridor clusters and settlement rails — match-type weighting, coin-type validation, false-positive handling, escalation paths.
- Stay current on the emerging typologies the corridor produces — bridge-exploit fund dispersal, privacy-coin conversion flows (shielded/transparent transitions), cross-chain hop obscuration, stolen-funds laundering-as-a-service — and convert them into detection and label logic.

### 3.3 Typology casework and court-grade reporting
- Conduct blockchain tracing casework during pilots: mule-network mapping, scam-centre fund-flow reconstruction, exchange off-ramp identification, mixer and peel-chain handling, cross-chain bridge hops.
- Produce tracing reports at brief and court grade (MY, UAE and CH flavours) with methodology statements, evidence and source citations, limitations, reproducibility appendices and a plain-language executive summary an investigator can forward unedited.
- Support law-enforcement design partners through their internal processes — investigation-paper support in the Malaysian flavour, jointly agreed cases, within agreed data boundaries.
- Build the report-template library from real cases; iterate against actual reader feedback at customer institutions.

### 3.4 Regulatory, FIU and law-enforcement engagement
- Serve as technical-compliance counterpart for Bank Negara Malaysia (Financial Integrity/Payments), Securities Commission Malaysia, VARA, SCA, the UAE FIU, Bahrain's CBB, Turkey's MASAK and SPK, Egypt's FRA and FIU, and Swiss FIU-class bodies.
- Author supervisory-grade artefacts: discussion papers, public-consultation responses arguing proportionate surveillance standards, baseline guidance (for example, "AML/CFT expectations for a newly licensed digital-asset exchange").
- Prepare and co-present capability briefings to investigator audiences on methodology and recorded public-data output.
- Support MENAFATF/FATF-adjacent positioning: Recommendation 15 coverage, Travel Rule obligations and implementation practice across the roster states.

### 3.5 Commercial, bid and enablement support
- Act as named AML/CFT SME on RFP/tender responses and supplier due-diligence questionnaires; own the reusable compliance-answers library.
- Review prospects' AML policies, travel-rule posture and tooling as the diagnostic hook of "day-one AML" bundles for newly licensed VASPs.
- Support pricing conversations on compliance substance — what a proportionate licence covers, what an address-band includes, why seat-based incumbent pricing misaligns for supervised SMBs.
- Deliver methodology workshops and investigator training during pilots.

### 3.6 Internal influence and product feedback
- Convert pilot friction into product backlog: data-handling gaps, report-format gaps, audit-log requirements, jurisdiction-specific features.
- Hold standing recommend-block authority in corpus and pilot reviews on unlawful data movement — the recommendation is recorded; engineering is required to answer.
