FAQ

How does Chainalysis Reactor trace cryptocurrency transactions?

Chainalysis Reactor traces cryptocurrency transactions across 27+ blockchains and 40 million+ assets, following funds through 325M+ swaps, 300+ bridges and DEXs, and the popular mixers within a single investigative workflow for end‑to‑end clarity.

It links on-chain activity to real-world entities using ground-truth attributions and rigorous clustering, producing reliable intelligence. These methods have been independently validated in peer-reviewed research as among the most accurate in the industry.

What makes Reactor different from other blockchain investigation tools?

Reactor combines comprehensive blockchain coverage with clear, flexible graphing visualizations that make complex investigations easy to follow. It leverages intelligent automation and advanced analytics to intuitively trace activity across chains, assets, and entities in a precise and efficient workflow.

It leverages Chainalysis intelligence that is independently validated, relied on by 1,500+ organizations worldwide, and proven in landmark cases that have led to the recovery and seizure of over $34B in crypto assets.

Who uses Reactor and what can they do with it?

Reactor is used by law enforcement, intelligence, regulatory and tax agencies, crypto exchanges, financial institutions, and corporate investigation teams to uncover illicit activity, trace assets across chains, link wallets to real-world entities, and present clear, defensible evidence.

For sensitive operations, flexible deployment options – including secure regional cloud residency, on-premises, and FedRAMP authorized environments – ensure mission-grade security and data control.

Can Reactor integrate with other investigation or compliance tools?

Yes. Reactor works with Chainalysis KYT enabling compliance teams to escalate risk-based alerts into full investigations with complete context and continuity.

It also connects with leading digital forensics and link-analysis platforms such as Cellebrite, Siren, and i2, uniting on-chain and off-chain evidence within one cohesive workflow.
