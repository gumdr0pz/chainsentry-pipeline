# Zetrix AI — Value-Transfer Thesis: Corroboration Pass

**Classification:** CONFIDENTIAL — internal assessment
**Date:** 2 November 2026
**Method:** five parallel verification wings, shared citation ledger, primary-filing preference

## BLUF

The core financial architecture is **independently corroborated**: a very large unamortised development-cost intangible, debt-financed, sitting at more than half of total assets.[1] The **governance collapse is heavily corroborated** — all seven regulatory items confirmed by two or more independent outlets.[1] The **SPAC structure exists and is confirmed in SEC primary filings**, but its headline valuation is **not**.[8][9][11] Three specific claims I made in the first pass require correction: the auditor's resignation date, the sponsor's ability to approve a combination alone, and the incorporation jurisdiction.

The thesis survives. Its evidentiary base is now split cleanly into what is provable and what is not — which is the point of the exercise.

That is corroboration of reporting, not of underlying accounts. The audited FY2025 statement was not retrieved.

The **25:1 capex-to-amortisation ratio** — the analytical heart of the reservoir argument — rests on those single-sourced figures.

**One variance flagged:** The Edge states FY2021 gross debt of RM166.1M; The Malaysian Reserve, via MARC, states RM160.5M.[1][5] An unexplained RM5.6M gap, probably a scope difference.

## 3. SPAC structure — CONFIRMED IN PRIMARY FILINGS, VALUATION NOT

This is where corroboration changed the picture most.

**Confirmed via SEC EDGAR primary filings** (CIK 0002097986):

- FTHAC is real and listed on Nasdaq (FTHA/FTHAU/FTHAW), file 001-43263; 8-A12B 29 April 2026; separate share/warrant trading from 22 June 2026.[8][9]
- **US$100M IPO on 1 May 2026** — 10,000,000 units at $10.00 plus 370,000 private units ($3.7M); 424B4 filed 1 May 2026.[11]
- Sponsor chain matches exactly: **Next Lion Sponsor Holdings LLC** (Delaware); **Next Lion Limited (BVI)** is sole and managing member; the prospectus names **"Yap Shon Leong… director of Next Lion Limited"** holding voting and investment discretion.[8]
- **3,688,333 sponsor shares = 26.9%**, verbatim in the 424B4 ownership table; at-risk capital $3.425M–$3.675M, consistent with the ~US$3.6M figure.[8]
- **1 November 2027 completion deadline** derived from the 10-Q's 18-month window from the 1 May 2026 close.[11]

**Correction — incorporation.** I wrote that Next Lion Ltd is "BVI" (correct) but the SPAC itself is **Cayman**-incorporated with an EDGAR location of Tortola. My earlier phrasing conflated the two.

**Correction — "sponsor votes alone can approve a combination".** I reported this as established. It is **not verified**. At 26.9% of ordinary shares the arithmetic does not support it absent super-voting shares, which were not found. Additionally, **500,000 founder shares were forfeited by 30 June 2026**, leaving 3,333,333 Class B — so the live sponsor stake is about **24.3%**, below the prospectus snapshot. My earlier statement overstated the control position.

**Not in any SEC filing:** Zetrix, the 25 May 2026 MoU, the 21 November 2026 exclusivity, "AI Foundation Lab", the **US$3B valuation**, or the **US$200M raise**. EDGAR full-text search returns zero hits for "AI Foundation Lab" across all filings. Those four figures remain **single-sourced to The Edge**.[1][2] A $3B target valuation attributed to a CLSA note is not the same thing as a disclosed transaction value.

## 4. Offshore revenue coincidence — PARTIALLY CORROBORATED

The underlying numbers are **double-sourced**. The Malaysian Reserve independently reports blockchain- and AI-related revenue of **RM127.6M (2023) → RM362.8M (2024) → RM643.5M (2025)** — exactly the amounts The Edge attributes to *both* the blockchain/AI segment and the overseas-geography split.[5]

But **no source other than The Edge frames or explains the equality itself**. The observation that overseas revenue equals blockchain/AI revenue remains a **single-source editorial characterisation**. The figures are solid; the interpretation is one journalist's.

**Minor transposition noted:** The Edge pairs RM127.6M (overseas) against RM126.6M (blockchain/AI) for 2023; The Malaysian Reserve reverses which is which. The pair of figures is the same; the attribution is transposed in secondary coverage.

**Named counterparties are real but their revenue status is unverified.** No customer-concentration disclosure exists anywhere accessible. What is documented — from deal announcements, not revenue disclosures — is a set of overwhelmingly **state-linked** partners: Guangxi Beitou IT Innovation (wholly-owned Guangxi state enterprise; flagship China-Asean AI Lab under a G2G MoU witnessed by the Guangxi governor and Malaysia's MITI minister; a reported US$1.38B Guangxi fund); CAICT and the PRC General Administration of Customs ("exclusive partnerships"); an international supernode on China's national Xinghuo blockchain; MBI, a Zetrix/MIMOS joint venture running Malaysia's national blockchain; and Philippines DICT/BIR/NBI/SEC.[6] Whether any of these is an actual **paying** customer of the RM643.5M line is unverified.

## 5. Counter-evidence — the strongest case against the thesis

A corroboration pass that only seeks confirmation is advocacy. The red-team wing found real disconfirming evidence:

- **STRONG — IFC invested.** The World Bank's private-sector arm put in **US$40M (~RM156M)** in primary equity on 25–26 February 2026 via ~**199.5M new shares** (implied ~78.2 sen), confirmed in two fetched full texts quoting World Bank Country Manager Judith Green and tied to the 13th Malaysia Plan. Money went **into the company**, not to insiders, after IFC's own diligence. My earlier "199.6M shares at 78 sen" is confirmed with trivial rounding variance. Caveat: only ~2.5% of the company, and the stock later fell to ~22 sen.
- **MODERATE — genuine national deployments.** Malaysia Blockchain Infrastructure launched 28–29 April 2025 as a venture with **MIMOS**, a government statutory R&D agency and not an affiliate, running Zetrix's Layer-1 chain; plus a MyDigital ID ecosystem partnership from January 2025.
- **MODERATE — the sell-down was forced.** TS Wong's disposals were **margin-call liquidations by banks** at declining prices, not discretionary insider selling. A founder destroying his own wealth is not the signature of profitable extraction.
- **MODERATE-WEAK — sovereign counterparty.** The Philippines selected Zetrix's blockchain for national public-blockchain infrastructure in July 2026, across six-plus outlets — but it is an MoU, and The Edge itself called the deal "off-then-on, puzzling".[3][4]
- **WEAK — sell-side coverage exists** and was partly constructive (Maybank IB), though it predates the collapse.

## 6. What the corroboration pass actually established

**Strengthened.** The balance-sheet architecture and the governance collapse. A RM3.7B intangible at 56% of assets, financed to RM2.2B of gross debt with RM541.6M of cash, is now a multi-source fact, not one journalist's reading. All seven regulatory events are corroborated.

**Weakened.** Three of my specific claims: the auditor date (wrong by roughly a week), the sponsor's unilateral approval power (unsupported and arithmetically doubtful), and the SPAC's incorporating jurisdiction (conflated). The US$3B valuation and US$200M raise are **not corroborated anywhere** and should be treated as a reported target, not a fact.

**Unchanged.** The central analytical judgement: the value-transfer *architecture* is real and documentable, and the benign reading — a genuine, state-backed, debt-financed technology build whose asset base is unverifiable from outside — is equally consistent with the same evidence. What separates them is a forensic audit of the capitalised invoices, which no public source can supply.

**No finding of fraud is made. No regulator has made one.**
