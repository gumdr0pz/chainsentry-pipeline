## 1. About Aras Integrasi and ChainSentry

Aras Integrasi Sdn Bhd is a Malaysian technology and security integrator with an established government and regulated-industry client base, including national security-agency deployments. **ChainSentry** is the company's sovereign blockchain-tracing and E-KYC product line.

ChainSentry provides jurisdiction-first blockchain forensics for governments, financial-intelligence units (FIUs), banks and licensed virtual-asset service providers (VASPs) that US-based cloud vendors price out, cannot serve due to data-residency or sanctions optics, or leave blind on Gulf–Southeast Asia–BRICS correspondent corridors.

The product's positioning is deliberate and is the standard against which this role's outputs are judged:

- **Sovereign deployment.** The customer's cases never leave the customer's jurisdiction. The product runs on-premises or in a jurisdiction-locked hosted instance, and the customer can audit the deployment.
- **Corridor coverage.** Measurable, sourced attribution depth on the USDT/TRON Gulf-to-SEA off-ramp graph and adjacent MENA/SEA corridors — the graph the incumbents underlabel.
- **Auditability.** Detection methodology, label provenance and per-corridor precision/recall are published, not proprietary. We expect every label to be defensible in the customer's court.

ChainSentry is currently assembling a design-partner cohort of approximately 120 candidate institutions across Malaysia, Vietnam, Thailand, the Philippines, Indonesia, Singapore, the UAE, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Jordan, Turkey, Egypt, Lebanon, Morocco and Switzerland. This cohort mixes regulators and FIUs, licensed exchanges and e-money institutions, banks and tokenisation platforms, and professional-services channels.

## 2. Why this role exists

ChainSentry's go-to-market is compliance-led. Regulators and supervised entities adopt a tracing tool only when three conditions hold: the methodology is defensible before their courts, the data handling satisfies their laws, and the vendor can sit across the table from an examiner as a credible peer. Those three conditions are a discipline, and today that discipline is carried informally by engineering and the product lead.

The company's named top program risk is that the commercial pipeline outruns the product: a large roster of well-known institutions creates expectation in small regulator circles, and unhandled compliance friction in early pilots converts goodwill into cautionary tales. This role is the primary mitigation on the compliance and credibility axis of that risk. It converts pilot agreements into lawful, evidence-preserving engagements; it makes the attribution corpus defensible; and it answers regulators in their own language, so the company's leadership is not the single point of financial-crime judgment across four time zones.

This role does **not** replace sales capacity; it makes sales compliant and pilots defensible. The distinction is deliberate and is reflected in the objectives below.

## 3. Role summary

Own the AML/CFT compliance posture of ChainSentry's alpha/design-partner program and its expansion: pilot contracting and data-governance terms, jurisdictional lawfulness of every engagement, sanctions and watchlist integrity of the attribution corpus, court-grade casework output, and the company's written presence before regulators and supervisors. The role is a practitioner position with program-ownership weight — hands-on casework and drafting in year one, with first-line analyst leadership as the cohort scales.

## 4. Key accountabilities

### 4.1 Design-partner program governance (first 90 days: heaviest lift)

- Draft, negotiate and administer the design-partner agreement suite: 90-day pilot term sheet, written-reference and quotation clauses, sanitised labelled-case contribution terms, and the **data-rights clause** — the provision that treats a pilot's labelled dispositions as paid consideration, not an afterthought.
- Maintain the pilot compliance matrix: for every active pilot, document the legal basis, the data categories crossing borders (if any), hosting location, retention schedule and destruction or return obligations.
- Pre-empt jurisdiction-specific blockers, including at minimum: Swiss banking-secrecy and data-localisation handling for FIU-type submissions (a Swiss-hosted instance is quoted as part of the product, not as an exception); UAE federal and emirate-level data rules across VARA and SCA regimes; Malaysian PDPA and AMLA 2001 handling for law-enforcement and reporting-institution data; Vietnamese and Egyptian pilot-licence documentation obligations.
- Run a structured compliance review with engineering before each pilot goes live, and a close-out review that certifies what corpus material may lawfully return to the shared corpus, in what sanitised form, under which clause.

### 4.2 Regulatory, FIU and law-enforcement engagement

- Act as the company's technical-compliance counterpart for central banks, securities regulators, FIUs and police financial-crime units across the target roster — including Bank Negara Malaysia (Financial Integrity / Payments), the Securities Commission Malaysia, VARA (Dubai), SCA (UAE federal), the UAE FIU, Bahrain's CBB, Turkey's MASAK and SPK, Egypt's FRA and FIU, and Swiss FIU-class bodies.
- Author supervisory-grade artefacts: discussion papers and co-authored supervisory whitepapers, public-consultation responses that argue proportionate surveillance standards, and baseline guidance such as "AML/CFT expectations for a newly licensed digital-asset exchange."
- Prepare and, where appropriate, co-present capability briefings to investigator audiences — methodology and recorded public-data output as the standard, with live demonstration scheduled only when the product's import pathway is demonstrable.
- Support MENAFATF/FATF-adjacent positioning: Recommendation 15 coverage, Travel Rule obligations and implementation practice in the corridor states on the roster.

### 4.3 Attribution-corpus and sanctions integrity

- Own the sanctioned-address and watchlist labelling pipeline: OFAC, EU, UK (HMG/OFSI) and UN lists, court-document addresses, ransomware payment trackers, and takedown archives converted into deterministic seed labels — versioned, dated and source-cited.
- Define label acceptance criteria and run QA on machine-generated clusters: no label enters the production corpus without a provenance chain that survives the question "where did this attribution come from?"
- Perform sanctions screening on corridor clusters and cross-border settlement rails for design partners that request it, with documented false-positive handling and escalation.
- Publish the precision/recall methodology: measurement per heuristic, per chain, per corridor, computed on the design cohort's own labelled feedback. This is a differentiator by strategy — auditable-but-young is positioned against opaque-but-old — and this role owns its credibility.
- Maintain the corpus compliance register: what data came from which pilot under which clause, and what has been sanitised to what standard.

### 4.4 Casework and court-grade reporting

- Conduct blockchain tracing casework during pilots: mule-network mapping, scam-centre fund-flow reconstruction, exchange off-ramp identification, mixer and peel-chain handling, cross-chain bridge hops.
- Produce tracing reports at brief and court grade — MY, UAE and CH report flavours — with methodology statement, evidence and source citations, limitations, reproducibility appendix and plain-language executive summary an investigator or examiner can forward without editing.
- Support law-enforcement design partners through their internal processes (for example Malaysian investigation-paper support on jointly agreed cases), within agreed data boundaries.
- Build the report template library from the first three real cases; iterate against feedback from actual readers at customer institutions.

### 4.5 Commercial and bid support

- Serve as the named AML/CFT subject-matter expert on RFP/tender responses and supplier due-diligence questionnaires; own the reusable compliance-answers library.
- Review prospective customers' AML policies, travel-rule posture and tooling as the diagnostic hook of the "day-one AML" bundles sold to newly licensed VASPs (Vietnam pilot cohort, Egypt first-approvals cohort).
- Support pricing conversations on compliance substance: what a proportionate licence covers, what an address-band includes, why seat-based incumbent pricing misaligns for supervised SMBs.
- Deliver methodology workshops and investigator training during pilots; the cohort's competence with the tool is a conversion driver.

### 4.6 Internal influence and product feedback

- Convert pilot friction into product backlog: data-handling gaps, report-format gaps, audit-log requirements, and jurisdiction-specific features enter the sprint plan through this role.
- Sit in weekly corpus reviews and pilot reviews with standing veto language on unlawful data movement — a recommendation this role makes, engineering is required to answer.

## 5. Authority and decision rights

- **Acceptance authority** on corpus labels and on sanitisation standards for returning pilot data.
- **Template authority** on the standard data-rights annex within parameters agreed with the COO; material deviations route to legal review.
- **Named SME signature** on compliance questionnaires and methodology publications, with product-lead countersignature on anything contractual.

## 9. First 90 days — structured objectives

**Days 1–30 — Ground.** Learn the product's gates and constraints (appliance deployment, Reactor/CSV import pathway, report generation, corpus versioning); study incumbent export formats; read the target roster and tiering with the product lead; inherit and critique the draft pilot terms; produce the jurisdictional legal map for the 15 seed-market targets with named blockers.

**Days 31–60 — Run.** Lead compliance review on the first three pilots end-to-end; stand up the sanctions/watchlist labelling pipeline v1; co-attend the first regulator or FIU engagement; draft the MY-flavoured court-grade report template from a real case.

**Days 61–90 — Publish.** Operationalise corpus QA acceptance criteria; draft the first public artefact (consultation response or methodology paper section) with the product lead; deliver the first pilot training workshop; present the compliance register and 90-day review to the COO and product lead with a resourcing view for the following two quarters.
